What this is
Ledger is a holistic finance and financial literacy platform. It tracks your net worth, everything you own against everything you owe, so you can check your real financial position before you make a decision, not after. The goal is to help you spend, save, and make money better.

Is my data safe? +
Ledger connects to your accounts through Plaid, the same connection technology used by thousands of financial apps. Ledger never sees or stores your bank login. Plaid handles that directly. The connection is read-only: nothing in this app can move money or make changes to your accounts.
What is the financial health score? +
A single 0-100 number combining four things: how many months your liquid cash would cover your fixed costs (runway), how much of your spending is discretionary versus fixed (elasticity), how leveraged you are (debt versus assets), and whether your net worth is trending up or down. It's a starting formula, not a certified financial rating.
Why don't I see a score yet? +
The score needs some transaction history to calculate elasticity and runway. Give it a few days after connecting a bank for that data to build up.
Can this move or send money? +
No. This is read-only. It can see balances and transactions, nothing more.

Equities and investments
What's a stock?
A stock represents part ownership, or equity, in a company. When you buy a share of common stock, you're entitled to any dividends the company declares and pays, and your return depends on how the company performs. There's no guaranteed return.
Source: Investor.gov (U.S. SEC)
What's a bond?
A bond is a debt obligation, essentially an IOU. When you buy a bond, you're lending money to the company or government that issued it. In return, the issuer commits to pay interest on the principal and, in most cases, return the principal when the bond matures.
Source: Investor.gov (U.S. SEC)
What's an ETF?
An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is an investment product registered with the SEC that pools money from many investors into a portfolio of stocks, bonds, or other assets. Each share represents your proportional ownership of that portfolio and the income it generates.
Source: Investor.gov (U.S. SEC)
What's a typical yearly return?
The S&P 500's historic average annual return is often cited at around 10%. Over the 30 years from January 1996 through December 2025, its average annual return was 10.4%. Any single year can swing sharply in either direction, and past performance doesn't guarantee future results.
Source: Fidelity
What's an IRA?
An IRA (individual retirement arrangement) is a tax-advantaged personal savings plan. A traditional IRA lets contributions be tax-deductible now, with withdrawals taxed later. A Roth IRA doesn't give you that upfront deduction, but qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free.
Source: IRS.gov

Understanding your numbers
Understanding your runway
Runway is how many months your liquid cash would last if you stopped earning today, covering only fixed costs. A longer runway means more room to handle a shock or take a risk.
Discretionary vs. fixed spending
Fixed costs (rent, minimum debt payments, insurance) are hard to cut quickly. Discretionary spending (dining, entertainment, shopping) is where you actually have flexibility if things get tight.
Why net worth, not income
Income shows what's coming in. Net worth shows what you've actually built, everything you own minus everything you owe. It's a more complete picture for big decisions.
